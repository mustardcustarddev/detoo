# 1. Describe work in user stories

Date: DATE

## Status

Accepted

## Story

As a member of the team, I want the work we plan described in user stories
kept in the repository, so that everyone can see who each piece of work is
for and why, and can talk it through before it is built.

## Acceptance criteria

* Each story is a Markdown file in this directory, numbered in sequence.
  Numbers are not reused.
* Each story says who it is for, what they need to do, and why: "As a [type of
  user], I [need/want/am required] to [do something], so that [reason or
  benefit]."
* Each story says what must be true for it to be complete, in its acceptance
  criteria.
* A new story is a draft. Its status records what becomes of it, such as
  ready, done or dropped.
* A story that replaces an earlier one, such as each of the smaller stories a
  large one is split into, says so, and so does the story it replaces. A story
  that changes what an earlier one asked for updates it, and both say so.

## Notes

A story is a reminder to have a conversation, not a complete specification.
Ron Jeffries described it in three words: the card, which is the short written
story; the conversation, in which the team comes to understand it; and the
confirmation, which is how the team knows it is done. Detail is added as the
story nears the work, and only as much as the next decision needs.

A story should be small enough for the team to finish in a sprint. A larger
one is an epic, and is split into smaller stories, each of which still
delivers something someone can use, before the work begins.

See Mike Cohn's guide to user stories:
https://www.mountaingoatsoftware.com/agile/user-stories
